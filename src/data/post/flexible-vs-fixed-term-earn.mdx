Most exchanges have two kinds of earn product for the same coin: flexible savings you can leave at any time, and fixed terms that lock your coins for a set number of days. For a stablecoin like USDT or USDC, the choice comes down to one question: is the extra rate worth not being able to touch your money?

## The short answer

- **Flexible:** withdraw whenever you like, usually within minutes. The rate is variable and can change daily.
- **Fixed term:** your coins stay locked until the end date. The rate is set when you subscribe and doesn't move. Leaving early, if it's allowed at all, usually costs you the interest.

## How flexible products work

You usually start earning the next day. Interest is worked out daily and on most platforms paid daily, either back into the product (so it compounds) or into your spot wallet.

The rate follows demand from borrowers, so it moves. A flexible USDT rate can sit at one level for weeks and then drop overnight when a promotion ends. Many flexible products are also tiered: the headline rate covers only the first slice of your deposit, say the first 500 or 2,000 USDT, and anything above that earns less. Our rate pages show those limits in the conditions column.

## How fixed terms work

You pick a term (7, 14, 30, 60, 90 or 120 days are common), and the rate stays the same until it ends. Before you subscribe, check these:

- Leaving early. Some products don't allow it. Others do, but you lose the interest earned so far, and a few charge a fee on top.
- When interest is paid: daily, or all at the end.
- The end of the term. Your coins usually go back to your spot wallet or into flexible savings. With auto-renew on, they're locked again for the same term at whatever the rate is that day, which may be lower.
- Caps and quotas. High fixed rates often come with a small limit per person, or a daily pool that sells out.

## Is fixed always higher?

No. A longer lock usually pays a bit more, but on stablecoins the gap is often small, and during a promotion the flexible rate can beat every fixed term on the same platform. A flexible rate on one exchange can also be higher than a 90-day term on another, so compare both before you lock anything. ## A worked example

The rates here are made up to keep the math simple.

Say you have 1,000 USDT for a month. A platform pays 4% APR on flexible savings, or 6% APR on a 30-day fixed term. For a short period, interest is amount × rate × days ÷ 365:

- Flexible: 1,000 × 4% × 30 ÷ 365 = about 3.29 USDT
- Fixed: 1,000 × 6% × 30 ÷ 365 = about 4.93 USDT

The lock earns about 1.64 USDT more. On 10,000 USDT it would be about 16.44 USDT a month.

Now say you need the money on day 20. On flexible you'd leave with about 2.19 USDT of interest. If the fixed term lets you out early but takes back the interest, you leave with nothing, so you'd have been 2.19 USDT better off on flexible. That's the trade: a dollar or two of extra interest against the chance you'll need the money sooner.

The flexible rate moves, too. If it fell from 4% to 3% after the first week, flexible would earn about 2.66 USDT for the month and the fixed term would look better. If it went up, the opposite.

You can run your own numbers in the [APR to APY calculator](/apr-to-apy-calculator) with compounding set to "None".

## What you give up by locking

You can't react while the term runs: if a better rate shows up somewhere else, or your stablecoin slips off its peg, your coins stay put until the end date. Rates can also rise while you're stuck at the old one.

Platform risk is the same either way: if an exchange freezes withdrawals, flexible and fixed balances are stuck together. Flexible just lets you leave when you see warning signs. See [is crypto earn safe?](/guides/is-crypto-earn-safe) for what has gone wrong before.

## Which one to choose

Flexible fits money you might need soon, including an emergency fund. If the fixed rate is only a little higher, flexible is usually the better deal once you count that freedom.

A fixed term can make sense for money you're sure you won't touch until the end date, on a platform you'd trust for that long. Work out the extra interest in dollars first, as in the example. If it comes to a few dollars a month, is that worth the lock?

Plenty of people split it: some on flexible as a buffer, the rest in a fixed term.

## Common questions

**Can the rate on a fixed term change?**
Not during the term. It's set when you subscribe. If the term auto-renews, the new term gets whatever the rate is on the renewal day.

**Does interest compound on a fixed term?**
Usually not during the term: the interest goes to your spot wallet or is paid all at the end. Over a month or less, compounding makes almost no difference anyway (see [APR vs APY](/guides/apr-vs-apy-crypto)).

**Is flexible safer than fixed?**
Both carry the same platform risk. Flexible only lets you leave sooner, and on a day when everyone is withdrawing, even flexible withdrawals can be slow.
