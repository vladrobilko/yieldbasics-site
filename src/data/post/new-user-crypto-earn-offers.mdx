Sign up for almost any crypto exchange and you will see it: **"New users: 600% APR on USDT."** Numbers like 100%, 555% or 600% look like a mistake next to the usual 3–8% on stablecoin savings. They are real, but they come with two limits that shrink the payout to a few dollars: a **small cap** on how much you can deposit, and a **very short term**.

This guide shows how to work out what an offer really pays, what conditions to look for, and when it's worth bothering.

## The math: rate × cap × days

APR is a yearly rate. A term of a few days earns only that fraction of a year, and only on the amount up to the cap:

**Reward = cap × APR × (days ÷ 365)**

You can also enter an offer into our [APR to APY calculator](/apr-to-apy-calculator) with compounding set to "None".

Take an offer of **600% APR for 2 days, up to 200 USDT**:

- 200 × 600% = 1,200 USDT per year, if it ran a full year (it doesn't)
- × 2 ÷ 365 = **about 6.58 USDT** for the whole offer

And **100% APR for 7 days, up to 100 USDT** pays about **1.92 USDT**. A bigger cap matters more than a bigger rate: 100% APR on 1,500 USDT for 3 days pays about **12.33 USDT**, almost twice the 600% offer.

### Compare it with a normal savings rate

A regular flexible product at **6% APR** pays about **3.29 USDT on 10,000 USDT over the same 2 days**, and it keeps paying after that. If you hold a larger balance, the everyday rate matters far more than any welcome offer. You can compare those on the [USDT](/rates/usdt) and [USDC](/rates/usdc) rate pages.

### APR, not APY

Over 2 to 7 days there is nothing to compound, so APY doesn't apply. Most of these offers quote APR, and the formula above is all you need.

## Conditions to check before you deposit

The headline is the best case. The terms usually add some of these:

- **"New user" can mean different things.** Some offers need an account opened in the last few days; others only a first deposit or a first Earn subscription. Older accounts usually don't qualify.
- **Identity verification (KYC).** Most exchanges require it before you can subscribe.
- **Limited quota.** Many offers are first come, first served each day and show "sold out" once the daily pool is used up.
- **The cap is per person**, across all your accounts. Anything above it earns the normal rate, or nothing.
- **What happens at the end.** Funds usually move back to your spot wallet or into a regular savings product at a normal rate. Some fixed terms can't be cancelled early, or you lose the interest if you do.
- **Payout timing.** Interest may be paid daily or only when the term ends, and sometimes as a voucher or bonus rather than withdrawable USDT.
- **Extra tasks.** Some exchanges tie the offer to a net deposit, a trading volume or holding their own token.

## Is it worth it?

It depends on what you were planning anyway.

**Worth a look if** you were going to open an account on that exchange anyway, for its regular rates or for trading. A few free dollars on top doesn't hurt.

**Probably not worth it if** you would open an account only for the bonus:

- **Network fees** to deposit and withdraw can cost around 1 USDT or more each way, depending on the network, which can eat most of a small reward.
- **Time:** sign-up, identity checks and moving funds take far longer than the reward is worth at a few dollars.
- **Platform risk:** your coins sit on a centralized exchange. If it freezes withdrawals or fails, a few dollars of interest won't matter. Spread larger amounts and only use platforms you trust.

## In short

- Divide the headline APR down: **cap × APR × days ÷ 365** gives the real reward.
- Rank offers by **total reward**, not rate; a big cap beats a big APR.
- Read the conditions: who counts as new, KYC, daily quota, what happens when the term ends.
- For real savings, the **everyday rate on your full balance** matters much more than a welcome bonus.
