Every crypto earn product shows a yearly rate, but some call it **APR** and others **APY**. They sound alike, and at low rates they're almost the same number. At high rates, or when you compare offers side by side, the difference matters.

## The short answer

- **APR (annual percentage rate)** is the simple yearly rate. Interest is calculated on your deposit only.
- **APY (annual percentage yield)** includes **compounding**: interest is added to your balance, and the next interest is calculated on the larger amount.

So for the same product, **APY is always equal to or higher than APR**. They're equal only when nothing compounds.

## A quick example

You deposit 1,000 USDT at **10% APR**:

- **No compounding:** you earn 100 USDT in a year. The APY is also 10%.
- **Compounded monthly:** each month you earn 10% ÷ 12 on a slightly bigger balance. After a year you have about 104.71 USDT of interest, so the APY is **10.47%**.
- **Compounded daily:** about 105.16 USDT, an APY of **10.52%**.

The formula behind it:

**APY = (1 + APR ÷ n)ⁿ − 1**, where _n_ is how many times a year interest is added (365 for daily, 12 for monthly).

## How big is the gap?

At typical savings rates the difference is small. At high rates it grows fast:

| APR  | APY, compounded monthly | APY, compounded daily |
| ---- | ----------------------- | --------------------- |
| 3%   | 3.04%                   | 3.05%                 |
| 5%   | 5.12%                   | 5.13%                 |
| 10%  | 10.47%                  | 10.52%                |
| 20%  | 21.94%                  | 22.13%                |
| 50%  | 63.21%                  | 64.82%                |
| 100% | 161.30%                 | 171.46%               |

For a 5% stablecoin rate, APR vs APY changes your yearly interest by about 0.1 percentage point. For a 50% or 100% rate, it changes everything, which is one reason some platforms prefer to advertise APY on high-yield products.

## Why it matters when comparing offers

Exchanges usually quote **APR** for savings products. DeFi protocols such as Aave usually show **APY**, because interest is added to your balance continuously. Comparing a 5% APY with a 5% APR isn't quite fair: the APR one pays slightly more if it also compounds.

To compare fairly, convert both to the same basis with the [APR to APY calculator](/apr-to-apy-calculator). Our [rates table](/) labels every offer with its type, so you can see which is which.

## When APY doesn't apply

APY assumes the rate stays the same for a whole year and that interest is reinvested. In crypto, that's often not true:

- **Flexible rates change**, sometimes daily. An APY calculated from today's rate is a projection, not a promise.
- **Compounding only happens if rewards go back into the product.** Some platforms add rewards to your earning balance automatically; others pay them to your spot wallet, where they earn nothing until you subscribe them again. Check how your product pays out.
- **Short terms barely compound.** A 7-day fixed term or a 2-day new-user offer pays simple interest on a small amount, so only the APR matters. See [what new-user offers really pay](/guides/new-user-crypto-earn-offers).
- **Tiered rates** pay the headline rate only on the first part of your deposit, so your real rate is lower than either the APR or the APY suggests for larger amounts.

## Common questions

**Is a higher APY always better?**
Not on its own. Check whether the rate applies to your whole amount, how long it lasts, whether you can withdraw at any time, and how much you trust the platform. See [is crypto earn safe?](/guides/is-crypto-earn-safe)

**Is the APY guaranteed?**
No. On flexible products the rate can change at any time, and the APY simply assumes today's rate lasts a year.

**Which number do exchanges show in their earn products?**
Most centralized exchanges show APR; many DeFi apps show APY. Read the label next to the number, not just the number.

**Does compounding change the amount of coins or their value?**
It increases the number of coins you hold. Their value in dollars still depends on the coin's price, so for anything other than a stablecoin, price changes usually matter far more than APR vs APY.

## In short

- **APR** = simple yearly rate; **APY** = with compounding. APY ≥ APR.
- At 3–10% the gap is small; at 50%+ it's large.
- Compare offers on the **same basis**, and remember that APY assumes a constant rate and reinvested rewards.
