A dollar in your wallet is still a dollar next year, but it buys less. That loss is **inflation**. When you earn interest on dollars or dollar stablecoins such as USDT and USDC, the number that tells you whether you're getting ahead is your **real yield**: the rate you earn minus inflation.

## What inflation does to cash

Inflation is the rise in prices over time. If prices rise 3% a year, $1,000 of cash buys what about $971 bought a year earlier. Over longer periods the loss adds up:

- At **3% inflation**, $1,000 kept as cash for 10 years buys what about **$744** buys today.
- At **4% inflation**, it buys what about **$676** buys today.

Stablecoins track the US dollar, so they lose purchasing power in exactly the same way. Holding USDT or USDC without earning anything is like keeping dollars under the mattress.

_The numbers in this guide are examples, not forecasts. Check the latest inflation figure for your own currency._

## Real yield: rate minus inflation

**Nominal rate** is the rate a platform shows you. **Real yield** is what's left after inflation:

**Real yield ≈ nominal rate − inflation**

The exact version is **(1 + rate) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1**, but the simple subtraction is close enough at normal rates.

With inflation at 3%:

| Nominal rate | Real yield (approx.) | What it means                |
| ------------ | -------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| 0%           | −2.9%                | Cash slowly loses value      |
| 2%           | −1.0%                | Still losing, just slower    |
| 5%           | +1.9%                | Growing in real terms        |
| 8%           | +4.9%                | Growing faster, at more risk |

A positive real yield means your savings grow in what they can buy, not just in number.

> **Try it:** the [APR to APY calculator](/apr-to-apy-calculator) shows what a rate earns on your amount over a year. Subtract inflation from the result to see your real gain.

## Don't forget taxes

In many countries interest is taxed, and tax comes out before inflation does its work. A rough after-tax real yield:

**Real yield after tax ≈ rate × (1 − tax rate) − inflation**

Example: a 5% rate, taxed at 20%, leaves 4%. With 3% inflation, the real gain is about **1%**, much less than the 5% headline. Tax rules differ a lot between countries, so check yours.

## Stablecoin earn vs bank savings vs T-bills

All three pay interest on dollars, so they face the same inflation. What differs is the risk you take for the rate.

**Bank savings accounts**

- Usually the lowest rate of the three.
- In many countries deposits are protected by a government scheme up to a limit (in the US, FDIC insurance).
- Simple, regulated, and easy to withdraw.

**US Treasury bills (T-bills)**

- Short-term loans to the US government, typically lasting from a few weeks to a year.
- Widely seen as one of the safest dollar assets; their rate follows the US central bank's policy rate.
- You buy them through a broker or the US Treasury, and your money is tied up until they mature unless you sell.

**Stablecoin earn (exchanges and DeFi)**

- Rates are often higher, and many products let you withdraw at any time.
- **No deposit insurance.** You carry platform risk (the exchange can fail or freeze withdrawals), smart-contract risk on DeFi, and the risk that the stablecoin loses its peg.
- Rates change often, and high headline rates may apply only up to a small amount.

The extra rate on stablecoins is mostly payment for those extra risks. A useful check: compare a stablecoin rate with what T-bills pay. If the gap is small, the extra risk may not be worth it. If it's very large, ask why. See [is crypto earn safe?](/guides/is-crypto-earn-safe) for the main risks.

## If your currency isn't the dollar

Dollar stablecoins earn in dollars. If you live where the local currency loses value faster than the dollar, holding dollars can protect you against that, which is why stablecoins are popular in high-inflation countries. The reverse is also true: if your currency strengthens against the dollar, your dollar earnings are worth less at home. Your real yield depends on **your** currency's inflation and exchange rate, not only on US inflation.

## What about BTC, ETH and other coins?

Real yield matters most for stablecoins, because their price is meant to stay flat. For coins such as BTC or ETH, price moves are usually far larger than any interest rate or inflation figure, so a few percent of yield on top is a bonus, not the main story.

## Common questions

**Is a 5% stablecoin rate beating inflation?**
If inflation is below 5% and you keep that rate for the whole year, yes, before tax. But flexible rates change, and tiered offers may pay the top rate only on part of your deposit.

**Can real yield be negative even with interest?**
Yes. Any rate below inflation leaves you with more dollars that buy less than before.

**Are T-bills better than stablecoin earn?**
They're lower risk. Whether the higher stablecoin rate is worth the extra risk is a personal choice. Many people split their savings between the two.

**Do stablecoins protect against inflation?**
No. They track the dollar, so they lose value to US inflation like cash does. Only the interest you earn can offset it.

## In short

- Inflation shrinks the buying power of dollars and dollar stablecoins.
- **Real yield ≈ rate − inflation**, and after tax it's lower still.
- Stablecoin earn often pays more than savings or T-bills, but without deposit insurance or government backing.
